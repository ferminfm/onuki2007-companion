\subsection*{Scope and evidence order}

The inspected 24-page Korteweg source addresses capillary forces in fluid
motion and gives its definitive component stress in Eq.~(20)
\citep{Korteweg1901}.  Onuki explicitly cites Korteweg in the historical
introduction to the dynamic van der Waals model \citep{Onuki2007PRE}.  The
evidence used here is ordered as follows: page-image source anchoring,
explicit separation of source and modern notation, the displayed
reconstruction, finite symbolic and dimensional checks, and only then a
comparison with Onuki. Bibliographic uncertainties about the inspected copy are
recorded in the source catalog rather than used as mathematical premises here.

Korteweg's source uses a pressure-positive component convention: the stress
divergence enters the momentum equation with a minus sign.  Onuki's pressure
tensor uses the same force orientation.  A modern tension-positive Cauchy stress
would instead be \(\sigma_{ij}=-p_{ij}\); inserting that optional sign map is
unnecessary when the two source tensors are compared directly.

\subsection*{Modern reconstruction of the capillary tensor}

Let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d\), let \(\rho,T\in C^2(\Omega)\), and write
\(q=\partial_k\rho\,\partial_k\rho\) and
\(\ell=\partial_k\partial_k\rho\).  The capillary block transcribed from
Korteweg's Eq.~(20), printed page 12, is
\begin{equation}
 P^K_{ij}
 =\left(\alpha_K q-\gamma_K\ell\right)\delta_{ij}
  +\beta_K(\partial_i\rho)(\partial_j\rho)
  -\delta_K\partial_i\partial_j\rho .
 \label{eq:korteweg-capillary-tensor}
\end{equation}
The four coefficients may depend on \((\rho,T)\).  Equation
\eqref{eq:korteweg-capillary-tensor} is a modern tensor arrangement of the
source components, not a quotation of modern notation from 1901.

For \(\rho,T\in C^3(\Omega)\), differentiate before collecting:
\begin{align}
 \partial_jP^K_{ij}
 &=\partial_i(\alpha_K q)-\partial_i(\gamma_K\ell)
   +\partial_j(\beta_K\rho_i\rho_j)
   -\partial_j(\delta_K\rho_{ij}) \nonumber\\
 &=q\,\partial_i\alpha_K-\ell\,\partial_i\gamma_K
   +\rho_i\rho_j\partial_j\beta_K-\rho_{ij}\partial_j\delta_K
   +(2\alpha_K+\beta_K)\rho_{ij}\rho_j \nonumber\\
 &\quad+\beta_K\rho_i\ell
   -(\gamma_K+\delta_K)\partial_i\ell .
 \label{eq:korteweg-capillary-divergence}
\end{align}
Here, for example,
\(\partial_i\alpha_K=(\partial\alpha_K/\partial\rho)_T\rho_i
+(\partial\alpha_K/\partial T)_\rho T_i\).  Dropping the second term is an
isothermal restriction, not an identity.

\subsection*{Conditional local map to Onuki}

With \(\rho=m_0n\), Onuki's capillary tensor from Eqs.~(2.47)--(2.48) may be
written
\begin{align}
 \Pi^G_{ij}
 &=p_1\delta_{ij}+M(\partial_in)(\partial_jn), \\
 p_1
 &=-\frac{M+nM_n|_T}{2}|\grad n|^2-Mn\Delta n
   +\frac{nK}{T}\grad n\cdot\grad T,
 \label{eq:onuki-capillary-expanded-for-korteweg}
\end{align}
where the second line uses \(M=CT+K\).  Select the Korteweg subfamily
and write
\[
 M_n|_T:=\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial n}\right)_T .
\]
This is a thermodynamic partial derivative at fixed temperature, not a spatial
or material derivative of the composite field \(M(n(\boldsymbol{x}),T(\boldsymbol{x}))\).
The coefficient restrictions are
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_K=-\frac{M+nM_n|_T}{2m_0^2},\qquad
 \beta_K=\frac{M}{m_0^2},\qquad
 \gamma_K=\frac{Mn}{m_0},\qquad
 \delta_K=0 .
 \label{eq:korteweg-onuki-map}
\end{equation}
Independent substitution into both tensors then gives
\begin{equation}
 \Pi^G_{ij}-P^K_{ij}
 =\frac{nK}{T}(\grad n\cdot\grad T)\delta_{ij}.
 \label{eq:korteweg-onuki-residual}
\end{equation}
Thus the source tensors are literally equal as local fields only under a
restriction that makes the right-hand side vanish.  Two sufficient,
source-supported cases emphasized here are an isothermal field and \(K=0\).
They are not an exhaustive list of pointwise zeros: for example,
\(\grad n\cdot\grad T=0\) at a field configuration is a kinematic condition,
not a constitutive model branch.  The \(K=0\) case includes Onuki's dimensional
simulation specialization \(K=0\), constant \(C\), but it does not identify the
printed Appendix-B B3 branch or unpublished simulation code.  For general
nonisothermal \(K\ne0\), the residual is generically nonzero; absorbing it into
pressure would require a separate equation-of-state and boundary audit.

\subsection*{What the map does not establish}

Korteweg's Eq.~(20) permits four independent coefficient functions and an
independent Hessian term; Onuki's mapped tensor is therefore a restricted
subfamily, not the whole Korteweg family.  No counterpart was found in the
inspected Korteweg source for Onuki's gradient entropy/energy functional,
generalized chemical potential, reversible entropy flux, alternative capillary
energy flux, wall functional, or global entropy balance.  Korteweg's static
wall data and Onuki's wall-transfer model are not interchangeable.  Local
tensor equality also does not establish an integrated balance or historical
derivation.  Korteweg's one-dimensional static relations in Eqs.~(34) and
(36), printed page 17, and the thin-layer reduction in Eqs.~(57)--(58), printed
pages 23--24, remain restricted structural comparisons unless pressure
normalization and boundary data are matched.
