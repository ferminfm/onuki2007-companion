\section{Source Section III.D: Two-Phase States in Heat Flow}
\label{sec:heat-flow-two-phase}

\sourceeqrange{Eq. (3.16), figures 5--7}

\subsection*{Purpose}

This subsection explains how to read the heat-flow droplet examples using the
source's own scalar estimates and boundary branches.  It does not reproduce
migration, spreading, or interface motion.

\subsection*{Reading Guide}

The temperature branch fixes different bottom and top temperatures with
insulating side walls.  Equation (3.16) is the main finite estimate: it defines
a characteristic velocity associated with latent-heat transport.  The relevant
fields are \(T\), \(v\), \(n\), pressure, and the generalized chemical potential,
but the companion uses them only as figure-reading labels unless a source
equation gives a finite identity.

For this scenario the latent-heat estimate is reused with the far-field heat
flux as input:
\begin{equation}
  v_c=\frac{Q}{n_gT\Delta s}.
  \sourceeqbadge{3.16}
  \label{eq:iii-two-phase-latent-velocity-type}
\end{equation}
The numerator is the imposed or measured heat flux through the heat-flow
branch; the denominator is the latent heat per unit volume associated with the
gas phase.  Thus Eq.~\eqref{eq:iii-two-phase-latent-velocity-type} is a scale
for reading interface transport.  It does not predict droplet migration speed
without the full hydrodynamic solution and boundary data.
The finite units check for this reuse of Eq.~(3.16) is covered by the
Section III mirror pair listed in Section~\ref{sec:numerical-results-overview}.

\subsection*{Boundary Branches}

The density wall condition from Eq. (3.2) remains active.  Heat enters or leaves
through top and bottom boundary data.  Side-wall heat flux is zero in this
branch.  Those statements do not close the global entropy or energy balance by
themselves; the Section II.D boundary-transfer terms remain necessary.

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{boundary object} & \text{scenario branch} & \text{what remains open} \\
\hline
\text{bottom/top }T & T_b\ne T_t & \text{global heat-transfer bookkeeping} \\
\text{side heat flux} & \text{insulating} & \text{wall entropy transfer} \\
\text{velocity} & \text{no-slip} & \text{no independent flow reconstruction} \\
\text{density} & \text{Eq. (3.2)} & \text{wall functional inherited from II.C}
\end{array}
\]

\subsection*{Limitations}

Droplet migration speed, contact with a wall, and subsequent spreading are
simulation outputs.  This companion records which source formulas the reader
should consult, but it does not infer a numerical mechanism from the figures.
